Lapsus$ Hacker Pleads Guilty to Rockstar Games Breach That Leaked 90 GTA 6 Videos
By
Tamilselvan
November 17, 2026
Categories:
Cyber Security News
Arion Kurtaj, the Lapsus$ hacker behind Rockstar Games’ 2022 breach, has pleaded guilty at Woolwich Crown Court after being released from a secure hospital and deemed fit to stand trial. 
The 21-year-old admitted offenses involving Rockstar, BT/EE, Revolut and Uber, alongside assaults committed in custody. His Rockstar intrusion exposed 90 unfinished Grand Theft Auto 6 videos containing nearly an hour of gameplay. 
The case highlights the distinction between Lapsus$’s historical 
data theft and extortion
 operations and ransomware campaigns that encrypt victims’ systems.
Lapsus$ Hacker Pleads Guilty to Rockstar Games Breach 
Kurtaj breached Rockstar while on bail for other hacking offenses and staying under police protection at a Travelodge in Bicester. 
Authorities confiscated his computer, but he used an Amazon Fire Stick connected to the hotel television, along with a Bluetooth keyboard and mouse, to carry out the intrusion. 
He then accessed Rockstar’s internal Slack environment and issued an ultimatum: contact him through Telegram within 24 hours, or he would release source code. The supplied account does not establish Rockstar’s initial access vector or identify a software exploit.
When Rockstar refused to cooperate, Kurtaj published stolen material under the username TeaPotUberHacker. The leaked footage exposed unfinished development builds and previously confidential details about the game, including its female playable protagonist.

recovery cost about $5 million, plus thousands of staff hours. The incident illustrates how development assets can become coercive leverage without file encryption. 
Once attackers obtain sensitive material, restoring systems cannot reverse disclosure, making access containment and protection of internal repositories operationally important alongside conventional recovery preparations and crisis communications.
Prosecutors also described social engineering across Kurtaj’s attacks against other companies. However, those findings should not be treated as proof that every victim experienced an identical compromise sequence. 
Related reporting describes how 
voice phishing and help desk impersonation
 can manipulate authentication and account recovery workflows, enabling attackers to obtain access through legitimate enterprise services. 
These techniques provide context for identity-focused intrusions, but the available Rockstar account does not establish credential harvesting, session token theft, multifactor authentication fatigue, or SIM swapping as its entry mechanism.
Kurtaj was previously considered unfit for a conventional criminal trial because of the severity of his autism. A 2023 trial of the facts therefore examined whether he committed the alleged acts rather than determining criminal intent. 
In December 2023, the court ordered his detention in a secure hospital unless doctors judged him no longer dangerous. A subsequent assessment found him fit to stand trial, and he moved to prison in January. He has now pleaded guilty, with sentencing scheduled for a later date.
For defenders, the case reinforces the need to secure identity recovery, restrict access to development assets and investigate anomalous collaboration platform activity. 
Phishing-resistant authentication
, including FIDO2 passkeys, addresses credential phishing risks, while carefully controlled recovery processes reduce opportunities for impersonation. 
These are broader defensive measures, not evidence of Rockstar’s specific failure. Kurtaj’s guilty pleas establish responsibility; they do not supply the missing technical details of the intrusion itself.
