International Law Enforcement Seizes Domain of Virtualine Bulletproof Hosting Service
October 27, 2026
|
Chainalysis Team
Summary
The FBI Washington Field Office, working with international law enforcement partners, seized the domain virtualine.net, which, according to the Justice Department, was used to facilitate prolific criminal activity.
Virtualine, a “bulletproof” hosting provider that openly advertised its services to criminals on cybercrime forums, hosted infrastructure tied to ransomware operators, infostealers, carding and credential-theft forums, scam operations, and the distribution of child sexual abuse material (CSAM), according to the government.
According to the Justice Department, Virtualine accepted direct, unhosted bitcoin payments from criminal customers and laundered the proceeds to conceal their origin, the government says.
On October 27, 2026, the U.S. Attorney’s Office for the Eastern District of Virginia and the FBI Washington Field Office 
announced the court-authorized seizure
 of virtualine.net, the primary web domain of Virtualine, a bulletproof hosting (BPH) provider that enabled a wide range of global cybercrime. The investigation was conducted with assistance from Germany’s Polizei Hessen, the Netherlands’ Politie Midden-Nederland, and the U.K.’s National Crime Agency.
The private sector also supported the investigation, and in collaboration with 
OverRIPE
, a coalition of independent security researchers and professionals focused on malicious hosting threats, Chainalysis provided intelligence support to international law enforcement in the disruption of Virtualine’s infrastructure.
What is bulletproof hosting?
Bulletproof hosting (BPH) providers
 are web and server hosting services that deliberately ignore abuse complaints, DMCA takedown notices, and law enforcement requests. By offering offshore infrastructure that resists disruption, BPH providers enable persistent criminal operations, including ransomware command-and-control servers, phishing kits, malware distribution, carding shops, infostealer logs, and illicit marketplaces.
Virtualine (also known as Secury, Massgod, Spamhouse, Lanedo, and Omegatech) openly advertised its services to criminals on cybercrime forums like Exploit.in and HackForums. In one September 2024 post on Exploit.in, Virtualine told a user: “You can use Virtualine for all illegal/black hat activities (including phishing, carding, email spam, port scanning and all others) except for child contents and terrorism activities.” In June 2025 on HackForums, the provider boasted that “Virtualine ignored more abuse than your last three therapists.”
In March 2026, Virtualine began marketing an “additional protection plan” that promised, upon receipt of a law enforcement request, to migrate a customer’s criminal infrastructure to a shell company unaffiliated with Virtualine before reactivating the server.
Despite publicly disclaiming the hosting of child sexual abuse material, prosecutors accuse Virtualine of knowingly hosting a criminal online forum that sold access to, sexually suggestive, and sexually explicit images and videos of minors and adults, and promoted a hack-for-hire service to illicitly obtain intimate content from victims’ accounts.
On-chain exposure to the global cybercrime ecosystem
Virtualine accepted direct Bitcoin payments through unhosted wallet addresses — a setup that allowed criminal customers to pay without triggering compliance checks at hosted exchanges. According to the Justice Department, Virtualine then laundered the proceeds to obscure their origin.

What this means for compliance teams
Today’s action underscores that bulletproof hosting providers are foundational infrastructure that allows ransomware, infostealers, and online fraud to scale. For crypto compliance teams, exposure to BPH payment addresses can be an early signal of broader downstream risk, as those same wallets frequently touch ransomware proceeds, scam infrastructure, and cybercrime marketplace flows.
